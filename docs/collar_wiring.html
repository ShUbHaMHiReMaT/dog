Breadboard wiring: power switch and marker button

Your small 4-leg button is a momentary button: it only connects while you press it. Use it as the marker button. For battery ON/OFF you need a slide switch that stays where you put it.

Before switching the battery on: check the chip on the HW-764 is not an AMS1117, measure its output (3.25-3.35 V), and never have the battery switch ON while the ESP32's USB cable is plugged in.

Step 1 · battery on / off

Slide switch in the battery line

The switch sits between the TP4056 OUT+ and the HW-764 VIN. Slide it one way: collar on. Slide it back: off.

+ (power)GND

Switch OFF before plugging USB into the ESP32. Battery and USB together would put two power sources on the same 3.3 V line.

Step 2 · your small button

Marker button on GPIO 14

Push the button across the middle gap of the breadboard. Use two diagonally opposite legs: one goes to GPIO 14, the other to GND. Diagonal legs are always on opposite sides of the switch, so you can't get it wrong.

  1. Press the button into holes e10, f10, e12, f12 so it straddles the gap (bend the legs straight if needed).
  2. Yellow wire: any hole in row 10 on the left side (for example a10) → GPIO 14.
  3. Black wire: any hole in row 12 on the right side (for example j12) → GND.
  4. The firmware enables the ESP32's internal pull-up, so no resistor is needed. Pressing it writes a "button" marker into the recording.
