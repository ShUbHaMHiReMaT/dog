Wrist trial: 36 minutes of live recording

ESP32-S3 + BMI270 motion sensor + INMP441 microphone, firmware 0.4.0, worn on the founder's wrist and streamed live to the laptop. Session MD_WRIST_20261022-170417.

The device recorded reliably for the whole session and is ready for the first dog trial once its sensor wires are soldered.

99.87% of motion samples arrived, the USB link never dropped, the microphone worked throughout, and every recording file was saved. The one data loss (2 seconds) happened while the device was being taken off the wrist: handling moved a jumper wire, and the firmware recovered on its own.

Watchdog

A second program checked the recording every 15 seconds for 30 minutes (120 checks). It never saw a disconnect. It caught the real wiring glitch at 27.5 minutes (4 checks during that minute). 74 warnings were "high resting jitter", a limit set for a dog collar that natural hand tremor exceeds; it is not a fault. The last 15 warnings came after the recording was stopped on purpose.

Before the dog trial

  1. Solder the four motion-sensor wires (3V3, GND, SDA, SCL) or fix them firmly. Both data losses so far came from these jumper wires being moved.
  2. Mount the device firmly on a chest harness, then press "calibrate standing" and "calibrate sitting" on the Live page.
  3. Add markers on the Live page as the dog walks, sits, lies down and shakes. Markers are what let us measure accuracy and tune the thresholds.
  4. Power from a USB power bank until the LiPo regulator is replaced with a low-dropout type (MCP1700 or HT7333).

Limits of this trial

  • A human wrist is not a dog's chest. Posture and breathing could not be tested.
  • The activity thresholds are still set from first principles and need labelled dog data.
  • A rotation of the device around the vertical axis cannot be detected by any accelerometer; it also does not affect posture.
  • The SD card is not detected yet, so all data currently goes to the laptop.
